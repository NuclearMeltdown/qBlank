qBlank vs NVIDIA App

The NVIDIA App records games on NVIDIA cards, with Instant Replay and an overlay. qBlank records a capture card, a screen or a window, on any graphics card.

The two projects

NVIDIA App

  • NVIDIA's app for drivers, game settings and the overlay on Alt+Z. It replaced GeForce Experience.
  • Free, closed source. Signing in is optional.
  • Recording runs on NVENC, so it needs an NVIDIA graphics card.

qBlank

  • Open source under GPLv3. Code, issues and releases are on GitHub.
  • English and German, with a manual in both.
  • One file of about 3 MB, from winget, the website or GitHub.
  • Runs on any graphics card. Encoders from NVIDIA, Intel and AMD, or the CPU.
  • Recording needs ffmpeg. qBlank offers to download it, from a fixed source, checked against its hash.
  • Updates are checked on start, if you want.

Capture card

  • The NVIDIA App has no input for a capture card. To record one, another program has to show it on the screen. Without a detected game, that needs Desktop capture.
  • qBlank opens the card itself. It fits the format to the video standard, deinterlaces what is interlaced, and has filters for composite and pixel art. See analogue consoles on the AmaRecTV page.
  • qBlank's window is an ordinary window. Recording it with the NVIDIA App works like with any other program.

NVIDIA App

  • It records the game it detects. Desktop capture adds the desktop and is off by default.
  • Protected content blocks recording.

qBlank

  • Screen takes a whole monitor, Window one window, games and video included.
  • A covered window comes through. A minimised one does not.
  • If the title changes or the window is gone, qBlank takes another window of the same program.
  • Each new picture is drawn at once. Recording → Frame rate on same as source keeps them all, or caps them.
  • System sound without qBlank records everything except qBlank's own playback. qBlank stays audible and nothing echoes.
  • Hide qBlank from the capture is on by default for the screen. It keeps qBlank out of every screen capture, Remote Play and Remote Desktop included.

NVIDIA App

  • Since 11.0.2 the default bitrate follows resolution, frame rate and codec.

qBlank

  • The encoder runs inside qBlank, through ffmpeg's libraries. With NVENC and AMF the frame goes from the GPU to the encoder without a copy.
  • A change of size or video standard during a recording starts a new file, seamlessly.
  • MKV stays playable after a crash.
  • Markers: Ctrl+Shift+F9 sets one, players show them as chapters.

  • qBlank keeps the replay in memory or on disk. The replay buffer and the recording share one encoder.

Screenshots and virtual camera

  • qBlank saves PNG, JPEG, JPEG XR or AVIF, HDR as JPEG XR or AVIF. Ctrl+F10 copies to the clipboard.
  • The NVIDIA App takes a screenshot with Alt+F1. Photo Mode, on Alt+F2, works in supported games.
  • qBlank has a virtual camera built in, a DirectShow source for 64-bit programs. The NVIDIA App has none.

Only in the NVIDIA App

  • Highlights: clips saved by themselves in supported games.
  • Photo Mode in supported games.
  • Finds games by itself.
  • Exclusive fullscreen with Windows' optimizations off.
  • A performance overlay, latency included.
  • Driver updates and game settings.

Only in qBlank

  • A capture card, with what consoles need: video standard, deinterlacing, composite filters, pixel grid.
  • Any graphics card. Encoders from NVIDIA, Intel and AMD, or the CPU.
  • One chosen window, even when covered.
  • MKV, which survives a crash, and AVI.
  • Lossless recording.
  • Inputs and outputs as sound sources, each with its own gain.
  • A 64-bit virtual camera with qBlank's full picture, HDR included.
  • qBlank hidden from its own screen capture.
  • Open source.
